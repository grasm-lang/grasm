Status

Every number here comes from the repository as the site is written, or out of the document that recorded the measurement. Five figures here used to be typed by hand instead. Four of them were wrong by the time anybody looked — the worst by a factor of two — and the fifth, “0 open defects”, was a claim no gate on this page makes. The scorecard has the eleven goals and where each one stands.

What holds

Interpreter, bundle and native builds agree byte for byte on stdout, exit code and diagnostics. A wasm build agrees on the 394 programs that do not need a filesystem, a socket, a process or threads; 84 are skipped because they do. 224 are also compiled for x86_64 and run under Rosetta, 195 of them agreeing and the rest declined. Every generated program is checked for what it keeps as well as for what it prints.

What is not claimed

Grasm is not faster than Rust, Go or Zig, and no benchmark here says it is. It is not battle-tested. Defects are still being found at roughly one per working session — falling, but not zero, and a language with zero known defects and no users has told you more about its test suite than about itself.

The no_std conversion that took a hello-world from 351.1 KB to 85.7 KB found nine defects on its way, and no gate on this page would have caught any of them: they were in the operating-system layer, below everything the corpus exercises. Two would have shipped silently — a variadic open passing a garbage mode, and an RwLock releasing the wrong lock. The list is on the scorecard.

How that is kept honest

All 98 findings are written down in ProjectGold/FINDINGS.md, including the ones that were the test harness's fault, the ones withdrawn as wrong, and the ones caught before they shipped. Every gate is verified by breaking the thing it guards and confirming it fails — and one of them had to be, because replaytest was matching 0 of 39 journalled builtins and exiting 0 for weeks, its pattern expecting a struct written on one line. It now asserts its own count, which is the check that would have caught it on day one.
