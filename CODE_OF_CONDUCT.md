# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about code, not
about people.

## What that means here

**Technical disagreement is the point.** Say a design is wrong and say why.
Being direct is welcome; being contemptuous is not. "This allocates in the
inner loop" is a review. "Did you even think about this?" is not.

**Be specific.** Vague criticism cannot be acted on, and it lands as an attack
even when it was not meant as one. Point at the line.

**Give people room to be wrong.** Several bugs in this repository were fixed
twice, and the commit log says so. That is how the work goes. Someone who feels
they cannot make a mistake in public will stop reporting the interesting ones.

**Not acceptable:** harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language,
sustained disruption, or publishing someone's private information. This holds in
issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the project happens.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainers privately. Reports are handled discreetly
and the reporter's identity is not shared without their agreement.

Responses run from a private word, to having a comment removed, to being blocked
from the project. Which one depends on severity and on whether it is a pattern.
Maintainers are held to this more strictly than contributors are, not less.
